### 1.2 Contract vocabulary (semantic, not wire enum)

- **Governing fact:** A qualified, attributed observation or decision within a domain owner's authority, limited by stated subject, basis and conditions.
- **Projection:** A consumer-facing representation of one or more governing facts (and optionally distinct attributed narrative), with qualification and disclosure limits retained.
- **Caller:** The effective human, agent or service actor whose request is being served, including any applicable delegation/lineage; the process transporting the request may have a distinct service identity.
- **Disclosure entitlement:** Qualified ability to inspect particular facts at a given time. It is **not** a mutable effect grant.
- **Effect grant / admission:** Domain-governed authority and effect-time decision that a proposed mutation may occur. It is **not** produced by a read or a browser button.
- **Attention item:** Attributed request/clarification/proposal that a human may inspect; it is not an owner-issued authority/eligibility determination.
- **Protected decision basis:** Owner-verified exact action, target, governing facts and preconditions needed to authorize a protected effect.
- **Intent:** Immutable action-specific proposed effect and precondition identity. **Admission/effect state** and **authorized observation or reconciliation operations** are distinct; neither approval nor result lookup proves remote effect completion.
- **Closure:** Source-qualified identity of the defined and observed *set* relevant to a conclusion, with epoch/coverage/comparability; not just the latest observed member versions.
- **Conformance:** Evidence qualified for its declared layer (schema/provider/client/composed/runtime); passing one layer does not assert the others.

## 7. Protected command and result-reconciliation contract

These clauses govern **semantics for a future qualified owner path**. They do not claim G2 or G3 exists. A client may create an untrusted action request; that is never effect admission.

### 7.1 Two independent dimensions: protected effect history and result-read attempts

The wire contract shall map each consequential intent to **two orthogonal semantic dimensions**, without prescribing global implementation technology:

1. **Protected admission/effect history:** proposed/no admission; refused *new admission*; admitted one-use; effect unknown/unresolved; effect observed (complete, partial, residual or confirmed cancellation where applicable); subsequently qualified correction/resolution. These describe owner-held facts, not the UI's most recent network response. A request receipt is not admission.
2. **Separately authorized observation/reconciliation attempt:** caller asks to inspect the same intent's state; the result is qualified disclosure of present owner-held knowledge, disclosure refusal/redaction or read unavailability. A successful lookup leaves the protected effect history unchanged, never refreshes approval and never dispatches another effect. Refusal to disclose an existing admission **is not** refusal or reversal of that admission.

These dimensions may change independently: multiple successful observations can still report an **unknown remote effect**, and read permission may be revoked before the effect resolves. The wire schema shall distinguish admission-record unknown, effect-result unknown and inability to disclose or retrieve a result, without inventing remote success. A multi-effect user workflow may have **correlation-only status**, never blanket admission/success: each governed intent retains its own owner history, partial effect and uncertainty, without invented global rollback. No universal cross-domain command service is required.

### 7.2 Minimum refusal and outcome distinctions

The wire specification shall distinguish, even where precise naming differs: invalid/unqualified caller or origin; disclosure denied or intentionally redacted **on result read** versus refused **new admission**; authority denied/revoked; unsupported capability; owner temporarily unavailable; stale governing precondition **including a changed displayed basis requiring fresh consent for authority-increasing/preserving effects or undisclosed extra protective targets**; different command under an existing idempotency identity; protected grant/generation mismatch; unresolved effect; validation failure; and a qualified effect/result. Refused attempts must not be mapped to success simply because the transport returned an HTTP success or accepted a queued proposal. Denial reason disclosure itself follows `AOI-03.c`.
