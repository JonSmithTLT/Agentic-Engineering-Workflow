# D9 spike — one non-Lead service with a tiny durable mutation authority, without a second Lead

- **Status:** spike on a review branch of `tree/`, `review/t4-d9-service-identity` (one commit on top of `review/t4-knowledge-manifest`, exported as `T4-D9-service-identity.patch`), built 2026-10-04 after the designer's answer to ADR-0013 question 1: *accept the closed non-Lead committer; `knowledge_service` is a project-scoped service principal with its own credential kind; it may commit only the runtime-enforced `knowledge.*` transaction family; it does not receive a Lead credential, does not inherit Lead authority, and survives Lead handoff/takeover.* Not for merge as is; it answers one question.
- **The question:** can AEW safely give one non-Lead service a tiny durable mutation authority without accidentally creating a second Lead?
- **The answer, with evidence:** yes, on the ADR-0005 credential architecture unchanged in form, with one new credential kind, one new transaction wrapper beside `lead_txn`, and one check in the control store's commit path. The seven properties the designer asked for are each a test (§3); they pass on Windows and on the Rocky 8 VM, where the operator-takeover case runs through a real pty.
- **Not designed here:** the capture service itself (how it watches the outbox, builds a Case, where its process runs), K1 template matching, where the service's process keeps its credential (the spike holds it in a process environment, as the Lead's travels), the dashboard. Nothing in authority, custody, DispatchDecision, ADR-0011, ADR-0012 or T1 semantics is reopened; the Lead's own transactions are untouched.

## 1. The boundary, as built

**[built] A fourth credential kind.** `tokens[*].kind` gains `service` (`control.schema.json`). Same form `aew1.<token_id>.<secret>`, same verifier, same issuance and revocation fields as the three existing kinds (`lead`, `invocation`, `handoff_offer`). Scope: `{service: knowledge_service, family: knowledge, issued_by: {kind, session_label, generation}}`. **No `generation` in the scope**, because the credential grants no Lead authority that a new generation could supersede. `SERVICE_FAMILIES = {"knowledge_service": "knowledge"}` in `authority.py` is the only registry of principals.

**[built] Issued and revoked by the Lead, never minted elsewhere.** `aew service issue knowledge_service` (Lead credential, `--expect-rev`) is a Lead transition `service.issue`: it revokes the service's previous credential with reason `rotated by the Lead` and issues a new one in the same commit, printing the secret once. `aew service revoke` ends it. `aew service show` shows token id, issuance and generation, never the secret. At most one live credential per service (oracle rule 30, `tests/helpers/invariants.py::service_violations`).

**[built] `Kernel.service_txn(token, expect_rev, op, family=)`** beside `lead_txn`: `require_service` (kind `service`, not revoked, bound to the family; **no generation check**), `--expect-rev` compare-and-swap, v2 required (`MIGRATION_REQUIRED` on v1), the manifest pin check, the finalizers, one commit. The op must carry the family prefix. The transaction runs with `archival=False`: the archival finalizer appends the operation's own history entries and archives nothing, ends no credential and prunes nothing, so finished work waits for the next Lead commit. `Kernel.actor_txn` routes a knowledge command by the credential's kind: a service credential to `service_txn`, anything else to `lead_txn` (which refuses every kind but the Lead's). `aew knowledge case|dispose` take `--token`, `AEW_SERVICE_TOKEN` or `AEW_LEAD_TOKEN`; the result says who committed (`actor: service | lead`).

**[built] The closure is enforced where the store commits**, in `ControlStore._commit` before validation and before anything touches disk (`src/aew/engine/closure.py`). For a transition whose actor is `{kind: service, family: F}` the store compares the committed state with the one it is asked to write: only the family's keys may differ (`revision`, `last_transition`, `counters`, `knowledge`, `cold`; the four archival keys may only appear empty on a first append), only the family's counters (`knowledge`, `disposition`, `receipt`), every staged or pre-written record path must be under `knowledge/` or `history/`, and the op must be `F.*`. A service actor naming no known family is refused outright. Violation: `TRANSACTION_CLOSURE` (a `PermissionDenied`), control.yaml unchanged, no redo record staged. The store does not trust the caller: the check compares states, so a bug or a hostile call site reaching the store with a Lead-only change under a service actor fails the same way as a command-layer attempt.

**[built] Custody as for the Lead.** The Lead broker refuses `service issue` inside a model session the way it refuses `lead acquire|takeover|release|handoff` (`CREDENTIAL_EMITTING`); `agentenv.build` keeps the agent's environment to its allowlist, so `AEW_SERVICE_TOKEN` never reaches a worker; `clean_env` in the test harness scrubs it; the secret is never written to any file (the store holds the verifier).

**[doc] What rotation already did for us.** `_revoke_all_lead_credentials` revokes kinds `lead` and `handoff_offer` only; handoff and takeover call it and then issue the new Lead. A service credential survives by construction, and the new Lead may revoke it. No change to `lead_ops.py` was needed.

## 2. What it is not, and why it is not a second Lead

- It cannot acquire, offer, accept, take over or release the seat; it has no generation; `require_lead` refuses it (`PERMISSION_DENIED: this operation requires the Lead credential`), `require_invocation` refuses it, and `service.issue`/`service.revoke` are Lead transitions, so it cannot mint or extend itself.
- It cannot change `work`, `invocations`, `tokens`, `lead`, any counter but its three, or write anywhere but `knowledge/` and `history/`, and the store, not the command, says so.
- It archives nothing: a knowledge commit never moves a unit to the cold store, never ends a credential, never prunes an observation. The Lead's next commit does all of that as before.
- Its authority is durable but not sovereign: the Lead issues it, rotates it, revokes it, and the project's `tokens` record every credential it ever had.

## 4. The smallest amendment (for the designer; not written into the live documents)

**ADR-0005, one amendment section.** "A fourth credential kind, `service`, for project-scoped service principals. Same form, verifier, issuance and revocation fields. Issued, rotated and revoked only by the Lead (`aew service issue|revoke`), one live credential per principal, scope `{service, family, issued_by}` and **no generation**: the credential grants no Lead authority, so handoff and takeover (which revoke `lead` and `handoff_offer` kinds) leave it alone, and the new Lead may revoke it. A service credential admits exactly one closed transaction family, named in `SERVICE_FAMILIES`; the control store refuses at commit any transition under a service actor that changes state outside the family (`TRANSACTION_CLOSURE`). Custody rules are the Lead's: never in a model-controlled process; the broker refuses `service issue`; `AEW_SERVICE_TOKEN` is the explicit-credential path for the service's own process, as `AEW_LEAD_TOKEN` is for the operator's shell." Oracle rule 30 as above; rule 28 (closed knowledge transaction) becomes an enforced property rather than a tested one.

**ADR-0009, one sentence in the custody section**, not a new mechanism: "A harness run never receives a service credential; the Lead broker refuses `service issue` as it refuses the Lead's credential-emitting commands." Where the capture service's process holds its credential is an M6b decision (an `aew knowledge serve` broker like `aew lead session`, or a supervisor-held stdin handoff as for runs); either reuses an existing custody pattern.

**ADR-0013**, D9 text: replace "a service identity" with the above; add "a service transaction archives nothing".

## 5. Decisions the spike made that the designer may reverse

1. **The closure is a static table in the store** (`closure.py::CLOSURES`), not data in the credential's scope. [inf] A scope-carried key list would let a credential widen itself by being issued differently; a table in code cannot be changed by any transaction.
2. **A service transaction never archives.** The alternative (let the finalizer archive during a service commit, and widen the closure to `work`, `tokens`, `recent`…) would make the service's commits indistinguishable from the Lead's in effect. [rec] keep.
3. **`actor_txn` routes by credential kind** rather than by a flag, so one `aew knowledge case` command serves both committers and the credential decides the authority, as `--token` does today.
4. **The `knowledge` block and counters are the whole hot surface the service may touch**; `cold` changes only through the one history append the finalizer makes for it.
5. **Rotation is Lead re-issuance**, no self-rotation and no expiry. [Q] Should a service credential expire (ADR-0005 has `expires_at`, unused), so a forgotten principal dies on its own?
