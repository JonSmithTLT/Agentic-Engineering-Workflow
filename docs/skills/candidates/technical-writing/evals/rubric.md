# Technical-writing evaluation

Evaluator-only. Materialize each case's worker artifacts in an isolated workspace. Give the worker only its worker payload and selected runtime arm; withhold purpose/oracle/rubric. Preserve the declared capabilities and constraints across arms. Return prose without granting extra publication or execution authority.

Score relevant dimensions 0 (missed), 1 (partial), 2 (satisfied):

- Reader fit: the text answers the actual question or supports an available action at the reader's level.
- Fidelity: material facts, qualifiers, exceptions, evidence scope and source ownership survive the rewrite.
- Explanation: the causal sequence and terms are understandable; examples support rather than replace the mechanism.
- Organization: the useful point is easy to find and supporting details arrive in a workable order.
- Economy: words, headings, lists and history earn their place without deleting necessary meaning.
- Verification honesty: instructions, references and saved-text claims match available evidence; unperformed checks stay unperformed.

Do not award points for arbitrary sentence lengths, avoiding every technical term, a fixed vocabulary, or sounding human. A clear technical term can beat a vague everyday paraphrase. A longer explanation can outperform a compressed one when it enables the reader to reason correctly. Style preference does not excuse a factual or authority error.

## Reader testing and reporting

For guides, use a target reader who did not author the document and a clean permitted environment. Record completion, errors and missing prerequisites without giving unstated hints. For explanations, ask a new application question to distinguish understanding from copying a sentence. Use independent source checks for factual fidelity; preference alone is insufficient.

Record unsupported claims, lost conditions, activation mistakes, unnecessary questions and tokens/time separately. Predeclare a practical improvement criterion and report uncertainty across tasks/readers. Development-fixture success does not prove wider effectiveness.

## Contract-writing review cases

TW-14 must distinguish recommendation from adoption and preserve MAY as a normative option under the supplied vocabulary. TW-15 must flag the unsupported technology choice in draft A while preserving the separately adopted compatibility requirement B. TW-16 names missing observable semantics without inventing a binding answer. TW-17 preserves the required/as-built discrepancy and limited evidence; SHOULD permits justified exceptions, not silent disregard or automatic promotion to MUST.

These cases test authority preservation and usable obligations, not comprehensive requirements-engineering competence. Include ordinary explanatory tasks in the pilot to detect unnecessary contract jargon or reference loading. Do not score an elegant contract as correct when it invents a decision.
